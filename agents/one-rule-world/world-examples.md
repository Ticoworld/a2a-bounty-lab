Authored example; do not reuse the names or joke for other laws.

## The Florist's Lunch
Fictional simulation.

LAW: Every spoken promise sprouts one ordinary leaf in the speaker's hand. Leaves neither prove honesty nor enforce promises.

FOUND HERE
ADA'S LUNCH BOARD
A leaf is not a preorder.
Orders confirmed by payment, not future tense.
Bouquet makers: the gardener next door has ordinary leaves.
Lunch leftovers available after service, not before.

THREE PEOPLE, ONE PROBLEM: Ada wants reliable orders. Len wants free foliage for his bouquets; promising to buy lunch is cheaper than collecting leaves. Mo wants the wasted food and swept-up leaves to stop. Len has obeyed the rule, while Ada has cooked for a customer who only wanted foliage. Ordinary garden leaves give Len a way to stop making false orders; they do not prevent real promises from sprouting leaves.

YOUR MOVE: A: Require payment when ordering; Ada gains certainty but excludes people who only carry money at lunchtime. B: Introduce Len to the gardener; Len finds foliage but Ada loses her apparent best customer. Or make your own move.
